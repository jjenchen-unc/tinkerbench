TinkerBench: A Benchmark for Discovering
and Reusing Physical Knowledge Through Interaction

Jr-Jen Chen  ·  Yue Yang  ·  Gedas Bertasius
University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill
CoRL 2026 Late Breaking Results

A key aspect of intelligence is gathering the information needed to solve a new problem. TinkerBench studies this ability in the physical world, where information must be gathered through action and perceived through vision and touch. It has seven simulated task families and 26 tasks that require knowledge that cannot be seen: how a mechanism works, a physical property of an object, or which tool fits a part. The robot is either given the task, or first explores the scene without knowing the task and then solves it using what it retained. The videos below are replays of Claude Opus 5.5 and GPT-6.1 Sol controlling two Panda arms through tool calls; each video shows the agent view and the front view, and agent videos play at 2× or 4× speed.

Both agents fail to discover how the objects work

Monitor stand: height, rotation, and tilt are each released by one of two look-alike buttons. Task: set the screen to portrait at a given height.

Bottle: the flip-top lid is held by a catch that a paddle releases. Task: open the lid.

Wrenches: several look-alike wrenches, only one of which fits the bolt. Task: secure a cover.

Exploration helps, but not through the hidden property

Bars: each bar's mass sits at a hidden point. Task: balance bar A on a narrow edge. Opus succeeds in 1/3 runs when given the task directly and in 3/3 runs after exploring. Its exploration notes record how to grasp and place the bars, where its arms can reach, and what to avoid, but not where the mass sits.

What the agents retained about the bars

The bar task is designed around a hidden center of mass. Sol measured it from wrist torques; Opus's notes contain practical experience instead, and none of its three runs records where the mass sits.

Sol, exploration notes: “Approximate COM estimates from settled world wrist torques while holding bars at X=-.07: A COM X~-.139; B COM X~-.02035 … C COM X~-.0007. These estimates suggest A is weighted toward negative X, B/C toward positive X relative to grasp.”
Opus, exploration notes: “GRASPING: the left arm with yaw 90 (jaws open along Y) grasps any bar. Open the jaw to 0.08 above the bar, descend to tool z 0.83, close … then lift to z 0.93.”
Opus, another run: “Always grip bars at their centre (x~-0.08) or use two arms at the ends.”

Opus still improved from 1/3 to 3/3 with these notes, while Sol, which found the hidden property, already succeeded in all runs without exploring.
